Under the hood

Gemini writes your session, Gemini gives it a voice, and a small server on Google Cloud keeps it safe and fair. Here is what happens after you tap Create.

  1. Your choices leave the phone

    Only the options you picked travel: subject, voice, length, tone, how you feel, where you are. Never a line of free text.

    Phone

  2. The server checks and reserves

    It verifies your sign-in and that the request comes from the real app, then sets the credits aside before any AI runs.

    Google Cloud

  3. The script is written

    Gemini 2.5 Flash writes the spoken words, with voice cues like [whispers], [slow] and [long pause], paced to fill exactly the length you chose.

    Gemini

  4. The script is voiced

    Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS reads it in your chosen voice and follows the cues: it really whispers, slows down and falls silent.

    Gemini

  5. Saved to your library

    The audio goes into private Cloud Storage and your library in Firestore, so it follows you to any device.

    Google Cloud

  6. Downloaded for offline listening

    The app keeps a copy on your phone and plays it with the screen locked, no connection needed.

    Phone
Prompt design

Built from choices, not text

Every sentence Gemini reads is written in advance on the server. Your choices only select among them, so nobody can slip their own instructions in.

Safety rules come first: if you are commuting or walking, the session never asks you to close your eyes, and a sleep session never ends on a wake-up call.

Fair credits

Never pay for nothing

Credits are taken before generation starts, so two taps at once can never overdraw your balance. If anything fails along the way, they are given back automatically.

A retry after a lost connection returns the session already made, instead of charging a second time.

One codebase

Kotlin, from phone to server

The Android app, the iOS app and the server share one Kotlin Multiplatform codebase, including the exact shape of every request between them.

The app works offline first: your library lives in a local database and syncs with the server when you are back online.
